The man who bet on Africa's farmers

The story of Mahesh Patel

and his book, Abundance: Growing Africa into a middle-income economy

Born in Nairobi in 1955, the son of a grocer who named his shop Bridge Traders, he spent more than four decades building a bridge of his own, between Africa's smallholder farmers and prosperity.

PrologueJuly 2016Afreximbank annual meeting, the Seychelles

That evening

Nearly 500 delegates had come to the Seychelles for the annual meeting of the African Export-Import Bank. The debate that animated the meeting was one that has long shaped Africa's future: should the continent industrialise, or should agriculture be its engine of growth?

That evening, as the sun set over the beach, Mahesh Patel turned to Professor Arun Tiwari, who was sitting beside him.

“There is no sectoral silver bullet for Africa's challenges. What we need is a paradigm shift—agriculture must move from simply producing food to becoming an engine of prosperity.”

Mahesh Patel, Preface to Abundance

Later, Dr Benedict Oramah, the bank's president, joined them. Being close to agriculture, he said, makes one realise that life's joys come mostly from relationships, friendships and working together, not from consuming and possessing things.

That was the evening the book was conceived. It would tell the story of a life spent in Africa's fields and markets, and of the farmers who, against all odds, still grow 80 per cent of the continent's food.

His life in nine moments

From Park Road to the rice fields

Keep scrolling to travel through eight decades, from a ship docking in Mombasa to a vision for 2050.

  1. 1946

    Mombasa, Kenya

    A ship from India

    Raojibhai Patel sails to Mombasa for the National Bank of India. By 1950 he has made Kenya home, with a shop called Bridge Traders.

  2. 1955

    Nairobi

    Born in Africa

    Mahesh Patel is born on 4 April, the second of four children. He grows up in Ngara and helps in his father's shop.

  3. 1978

    Nairobi and beyond

    Learning the forest

    He joins Export Finance Company and starts travelling: Tanzania, Uganda, Congo, Sudan, Somalia, Ethiopia.

  4. 1982

    Nairobi

    The day everything was taken

    During an attempted coup, his father's shop is looted and gutted. He keeps his word and buys EFC anyway.

  5. 1983

    East Africa

    ETG is born

    EFC becomes Export Trading Group, selling East Africa's own harvests to the food-aid agencies that once shipped food in from Europe.

  6. 1984

    Korem, Ethiopia

    A famine he never forgot

    What he sees on the road from Addis Ababa convinces him that Africa's land must feed its people first.

  7. 2006

    Kapunga, Tanzania

    The farm nobody trusted

    He takes over a ruined state rice farm, returns 2,350 hectares to villagers and builds an outgrower scheme.

  8. 2018

    Kilifi, Kenya

    Cashew is life

    Korosho Ni Maisha begins. By 2025, more than a million cashew seedlings are in the hands of 30,000 families.

  9. 2025

    Dar es Salaam

    Abundance

    He signs the preface of the book that tells it all, and lays out a vision for Africa in 2050.

The book that tells the whole story
