Agriculture must become Africa's engine of prosperity

For most smallholders, farming is still a means of survival. The vision in Abundance is of farms that earn, rural economies that grow, and young people who choose to stay.

Why the farmer is not the problem

Connect the system, and the same farmer prospers

Interventions designed one at a time rarely add up. The vision asks for an ecosystem planned together, run on commercial terms, and judged by whether farmers become more prosperous.

Seed without water. A loan without a buyer. Each service arrives alone, on its own timetable, and the farmer carries all of the risk.

Seven services planned together and run on commercial terms. The same farmer, on the same land, becomes an enterprise.

The vision

Five shifts from survival to prosperity

Each shift replaces a pattern that has kept African agriculture small with one that lets it compound. Keep scrolling to move through them.

  1. Shift 1 of 5

    From Subsistence farming

    Commercially sustainable smallholder enterprises

    A farm that earns a surplus can invest, hire and grow. One that only feeds the household cannot.

  2. Shift 2 of 5

    From Fragmented interventions

    A coordinated farmer-centric ecosystem

    Inputs, agronomy, irrigation, finance, mechanisation, processing and markets planned together, not project by project.

  3. Shift 3 of 5

    From Dependence on short-term support

    Long-term institutions and patient capital

    Transformation takes years. Institutions and investors must be built to stay through the early, unprofitable seasons.

  4. Shift 4 of 5

    From Raw commodity supply

    Processing, value addition and integrated markets

    Milling, storage and processing near the farm keep value, jobs and income in Africa's rural economies.

  5. Shift 5 of 5

    From Rural poverty and migration

    Vibrant rural economies with opportunities for young people

    When farming pays, young people and women have reasons to build their futures where they are.

The manifesto

Vision 2050: from rural poverty to shared prosperity

Part III of Abundance connects the lessons from Kapunga to prosperous agricultural economies across Sub-Saharan Africa, and to Africa's path to middle-income status.

  • Livelihoods

    Farm incomes that cover school fees, health care and reinvestment, not only the next season's food.

  • Youth and women's economic participation

    Agriculture as a career young people choose, and full participation for the women who already do much of the work.

  • Agronomic capability

    Extension, trained agronomists and demonstration plots that turn good practice into habit. Seeing is believing.

  • Resilient food systems

    Irrigation, storage and diversified crops that hold up through drought, price shocks and disruption.

  • Growing domestic markets

    Processing and value addition close to the farm, so that African demand is met by African production.
